## 2. Training safely

Sprinting is hard on the body. Sprint doesn't give medical advice, and nothing in it, including your readiness score, heart-rate zones, recovery numbers or cardio fitness, is a diagnosis or a reason to train, or not to train. If you have a health condition, or you're unsure whether intense exercise is right for you, talk to a doctor first.

- Choose a surface and a place that are safe, and warm up properly.
- Look where you're running, not at your wrist or your phone.
- Stop if you feel unwell. A plan in the app is never a reason to push on.

You are responsible for how you train and for the places you train in.

## 3. Estimates, not measurements

Sprint works out distance and speed from your Watch's GPS and motion sensors. These can be wrong, especially near buildings, trees or stands, or on a short straight. The wind comes from a weather forecast from Apple Weather, not from an instrument beside your track. The wind-legal warning is a guide to whether a day is fast, not an official reading.

Don't use Sprint's times, distances or wind figures as proof of a performance, or for a record, a ranking, a selection or a competition result. Use the timing and wind equipment the event provides.

You are responsible for your own backups. If you delete a session, or choose Delete all data, it can't be recovered through us, because we don't have a copy. You can export your sessions at any time from Settings.
